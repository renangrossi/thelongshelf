A researched personal-library catalogue

The Long Shelf526 catalogue entries across two collections. 673 physical books. 300,954 pages, taken from print editions rather than ebook files.

Two shelves: a 352-entry fiction library and a 174-entry world history canon. Every entry gets a representative print edition; every collection, omnibus and multi-volume work is opened up into the books it actually contains; every series is pulled back together.

      Method and sources

      What was checked, and what wasn't. A handful of things were confirmed against live sources: the First Law omnibus (Orbit 2016, 1,680 pp), the composition of Asimov's Foundation Novels bundle, the contents of Haggard's Ultimate Collection, the seventeen-title Sackett canon, the Zondervan Story edition, the Bloomsbury Enuma Elish (2024, 352 pp), and the two-volume structure of Seth's Korea.

      Every other page count comes from bibliographic knowledge of standard editions rather than a record retrieved today. They are good working figures, not audited ones. ISBNs are left blank rather than invented. Checking all 673 books against publisher records individually is a much larger job than one pass — batching by author or by canon section is the honest route.

      How editions were selected

      For modern fiction: the standard full-text paperback from the original publisher in its home market. For classics and translated works: a reputable complete edition, usually Penguin Classics or Oxford World's Classics, with the translator named, because the translator often changes the page count more than the publisher does. For the history canon: the edition a reader would actually be sold today — the current university press paperback, or the standard trade hardcover where no paperback exists.

      Why page counts differ between editions

      Trim size, type size, leading and margin move the number before a word changes. Apparatus adds more: introductions, notes, appendices and maps can be a hundred pages. Then there are books that exist in genuinely different texts — The Stand, Stranger in a Strange Land, The Gunslinger, The Forever War and Roadside Picnic on the fiction shelf; Gibbon, Froissart, Díaz del Castillo and the one-volume Three Kingdoms on the history shelf, all of which circulate in abridgements that cut most of the book. Each is flagged in its Notes.

      Sources ranked

      Publisher catalogue records first, then national library and ISBN records, then WorldCat, then Google Books. Retail listings were used only to corroborate — their page counts are frequently an ebook's estimated length rather than a printed edition's, which is exactly the number this catalogue was built to avoid.

      On the Goodreads ratings

      Ratings are real lookups or nothing. There is no way to recall a number like 4.29 out of 1.64 million ratings from memory without inventing it, so the column is blank wherever a rating was not actually retrieved — 36 books out of 569, which is honest partial coverage rather than a full pass. Goodreads has no bulk lookup, so completing it means roughly one query per title.

      Two faster routes: if the library is already on Goodreads, the site's own CSV export (Account Settings → Import/Export) returns every shelved book with its average rating in one file, which can be joined straight onto this catalogue. Failing that, the ratings can be filled in batches. Note also that ratings drift daily and that omnibus editions carry their own separate rating, usually higher and with far fewer votes: the First Law omnibus sits at 4.53 from about 11,000 ratings while The Blade Itself alone is 4.22 from 316,000.

      On the awards

      Awards are book-level and restricted to wins, not nominations — a shelf where everything is a "Hugo nominee" tells you nothing. Where an omnibus entry has no award of its own but contains books that do, the row shows the component awards marked via components, so the Hyperion Cantos file inherits Hyperion's Hugo without pretending the bundle won anything.

      Author-level honours are kept visibly separate and greyed: Steinbeck, Golding and Ishiguro have Nobel Prizes, but the Nobel is for a body of work, not for Of Mice and Men. Two cases worth knowing: A. B. Guthrie won the Pulitzer for The Way West, the sequel, not for The Big Sky which is the book on the shelf; and Asimov's Hugo for Best All-Time Series went to the original Foundation trilogy as a unit, which is why it sits on Foundation rather than on any one volume.

      Where a count could not be established

      • Nova Roma and Nova Roma 2 (Gentry Anderson) and the two Aron Beauregard titles are independently published with no stable print record. Left blank rather than guessed.
      • Haggard's Ultimate Collection holds 60+ works. Eighteen major novels are costed; the rest is short fiction, essays and autobiography, so its component total is a floor rather than a sum.
      • The Complete Wheel of Time — the fourteen main novels are certain; whether the file also carries the prequel New Spring cannot be told from the filename.
      • Wolfe's "1-5 The Book of the New Sun" is read as the four core volumes plus The Urth of the New Sun, on the strength of the numbering and the Long Sun files running 6 to 9 in the same library.
      • The two Portuguese Quixote volumes — the translator is not identifiable from the filename, and Brazilian and Portuguese editions differ substantially in length.
      • Sophocles — Oedipus the King and Antigone are almost never printed as a pair. The figure given is those two plays inside The Three Theban Plays, not a real standalone edition.
      • Constantine VII's De Administrando Imperio exists in English essentially only as the Dumbarton Oaks scholarly edition — expensive, hard to find, and a very different reading experience from the rest of the Byzantium section.

      Collections where the breakdown is estimated

      Barsoom, Pellucidar, the Sacketts and the Alatriste novels are short books issued by many reprint houses at different trim sizes, so component counts are typical rather than definitive. The same applies to Gibbon, Three Kingdoms and The Story of the Stone. The Once and Future King lists its four constituent books with no per-book counts, because White revised them substantially when he assembled the omnibus and the parts are not separately in print.

      Two things on the fiction shelf that are not fiction

      1 Enoch: The Hermeneia Translation is a scholarly translation of an ancient apocryphal text, and The Story is a chronologically rearranged abridgement of the NIV Bible. Both sit in the fiction folder, both are listed in the table, and neither is counted toward the fiction page total.

      One title renamed

      Agatha Christie's 1939 novel appears in the fiction list under its original UK title, which contains a racial slur and has not been used in print since the 1980s. It is catalogued here as And Then There Were None.
